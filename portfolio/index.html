About

Running has shaped almost everything about how I approach my academic work: it taught me to trust a process, to pay attention to data on my own body, and to take recovery as seriously as effort. Training at the Division I level also showed me, firsthand, how easily the line between "pushing hard" and "pushing too far" can blur — which is part of why I was drawn to writing about overtraining and mental health for this portfolio.

At the same time, I'm interested in questions that have nothing to do with a stopwatch. Working with a research group on how race, education, and career intersect with class identity pushed me to think about a different kind of system — one where effort and achievement don't always convert into the outcomes they're supposed to. Both projects, in different ways, are about the same thing: what happens when you look closely at whether hard work is actually being rewarded fairly, whether that's by a training plan or by an economy.

The three artifacts below represent that range — a set of applied, public-facing infographics; a literature review grounded in my own experience as an athlete; and a collaborative research-based literature review on race, education, and economic mobility.
